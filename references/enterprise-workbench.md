Adapt its information hierarchy and restrained surfaces; do not copy depicted brands, personal identities, financial figures, or decorative charts into the user's product.

## Start from a business question

This direction suits a homepage that brings several useful signals together: project management, operations, service administration, and other recurring enterprise work. A backend detail page, editor, or focused form does not need to become a dashboard merely because it is a B-end product.

Organize a genuine overview around primary signals, relevant trends/comparisons, supporting records/tasks, and secondary shortcuts. This is a reasoning order, not a fixed four-row template. Urgent exceptions or the next required action may deserve the first position. Explain scope, period, units, comparison basis, and data freshness; keep summaries connected to their evidence.

## Visual direction

- Use near-white content surfaces on a quiet neutral canvas, consistent insets/gutters, related radii, and fine edges or very light elevation.
- Keep navigation stable and scannable. A sidebar, compact rail, or top navigation should follow the product's object model rather than imitate the screenshot.
- Bound comparable content and compact tools in cards; a table or related task area can remain unframed. Whitespace should clarify groups without wasting the expert user's scan space.
- Concentrate action color in the current context and the main action. The reference's navy/purple and teal are one palette option, not mandatory replacements for blue-white or an existing brand.
- Treat chart series and state semantics separately. Green/teal is not automatically proof of favorable business change. Retain readable foreground/background pairs and textual status labels.

## Charts support decisions

Choose a chart from the question: lines for time patterns, bars for comparisons, a suitable part-to-whole view for composition, and a table for record-level evidence. A ring is optional, not the default for every percentage. Prefer a simpler comparison when small differences matter.

Avoid decorative mixed chart types, unlabeled axes, arbitrary metrics, or fabricated trends. Keep units, filters, totals, and time scopes consistent across summaries and details. Provide labels and accessible data where needed; don't rely only on hue.

## Typography and adaptability

Use [font selection](font-selection.md) to compare the user's supported UI/mono choices on actual navigation, table, field, and technical-value samples. Match type density to the work; a larger hero title is not a substitute for hierarchy. Keep numeric columns aligned and reserve monospace for a deliberate role.

On mobile, prioritize the next task and essential signals, move secondary tools into context, and preserve table comparison through a deliberate list/detail or bounded-scroll pattern. Dark mode needs its own surfaces, boundaries, chart labels, and states. Recheck typography and wrapping after font changes in both languages and modes.

## Recommend and verify

Present a project-specific slice with representative data marked as illustrative when necessary. Compare it with another credible composition only when the user's choice is still open. Keep controls functional, inspect overlays and empty/error states, and verify that the primary workflow remains clear at narrow and wide sizes. Do not infer performance or interaction quality from a static reference image.
