# Project-specific art direction

Design a recognizable, coherent whole before assembling components. This applies to a dense tool as well as a portfolio. A neutral interface is valid when it is a deliberate answer to the brief; it is not the automatic definition of quality. Avoid substituting extra gradients, more saturation or more parameters for a design idea.

Use blue-white by default unless the user explicitly requires a different palette, a required brand constraint applies, or a design has already been confirmed. Preserve that exception rather than resetting it. Distinction normally comes first from composition, typography, useful media and content hierarchy; it does not require changing the default hue or adding a warm/pastel theme.

## Make the visual decision

Read the actual project and user intent. Infer the audience, core task, content shape, usage frequency and constraints. Choose a concept that can be shown: a working notebook, a precise instrument panel, a visual catalog, an editorial journal or another project-grounded direction. Do not map an industry to one universal color or invent business evidence to support the concept.

Capture a short `designIntent`:

| Field | What must be concrete |
|---|---|
| `concept` | What the interface should feel like, and why that helps this task |
| `signature` | At least two visible features, such as a dark forest navigation rail, warm continuous reading surface, large section numerals or image/contact-sheet composition |
| `colorPlan` | Canvas, reading/data surface, navigation, visual anchor, companion, action and semantic roles; where color is used |
| `typePlan` | Display/body contrast, weight, measure and real available Chinese/English fonts |
| `layout` | Main content, context, navigation and next action; what earns space and what should be absent |
| `imagery` | Real assets or purposeful illustration/data marks, crop and contrast; “none” is valid |
| `avoid` | Concrete regressions, such as a generic KPI-card hero, faint text or replacing the primary document with decorative charts |

This is an internal design decision, not a form the user must complete. The simple entry still shows a recommendation, a few alternatives and a clear confirmation. Detailed controls stay closed by default. A scoped fix or accepted design does not restart style discovery.

## Color is a relationship across the page

Three color roles do not mean white/gray plus one button color. Structure may be warm, cool, tinted or split into dark and light regions. Brand color can occupy navigation, a heading region, editorial feature, selected object or a meaningful illustration. Reading surfaces can remain quiet without forcing the entire composition to be pale.

Prepare complete foreground/background pairs in both appearances. Give a brand companion a specific non-status job, such as collection covers or a featured context panel. Keep success, warning and destructive meaning stable, labeled and distinct from decoration. No universal 60/30/10 ratio, minimum number of hues or maximum colored area applies to every product.

The default blue family uses cobalt and ice-white relationships; forest/lime, plum/apricot, clay/oat, graphite/ice and wine/mint remain optional families for requested or selected directions. They provide anchor, on-anchor, companion, on-companion and canvas roles alongside action and state tokens. They are not an exhaustive aesthetic menu. `colorPlacement` compares contained accents, a brand anchor and paired regions. It is independent of density, material and appearance. Keep normal semantic colors labeled; a blue-white theme does not turn success or warning into blue.

## Density and expression are independent

“Management”, “table”, “course”, “reading” and “clear” describe content needs, not a request to remove identity. A dense console can have a confident colored shell and crisp typography while keeping legible rows. A reading product can have a strong masthead and expressive cover art while keeping paragraphs calm. Respond to explicit visual preference without sacrificing its tasks.

Vary the hierarchy of regions and the scale of type before adding decoration. Related actions, icons and controls still share one visual family. Choose card boundaries, open sections, lists and table surfaces according to content; do not frame every group or enlarge sparse metrics to make a layout look complete. Shape and motion have purpose, rather than every panel sharing the same pill corner or float shadow.

## Offer coherent alternatives

For an unselected Demo route, prepare one recommendation and up to two credible alternatives on the same representative content. Differences should include at least one meaningful compositional change and a coordinated change in type or color distribution. Explain the task benefit and a real compromise in everyday language. Keep accepted brand constraints; when hue is fixed, distinguish composition, scale, surface treatment, image handling and rhythm.

The built-in four composition IDs are reusable seeds. They do not dictate the project's identity or force a KPI/chart/table dashboard. Reuse useful components, rearrange or omit irrelevant modules, and use project-authored scenes when the sample cannot express the concept. Do not invent unrelated data or build three full applications merely to prove difference.

Natural-language feedback is a revision request: “old-fashioned”, “too pale”, “too much like a backend”, or “more like a magazine” needs diagnosis of the existing composition, type and color relationships. Revise a coherent set, preserve explicit choices, show the result and state the visible difference. Do not make the user choose hex values or use a keyword-to-theme switch as a substitute for judgment.

## Keep intention through implementation

Store the current project and direction intent in the confirmation JSON and optional exported prompt alongside exact light/dark tokens, current manual adjustments and project scene/media references. In the same conversation, implement directly from the confirmed scene and project source. An optional handoff must preserve why the layout looks this way, not only radius and font IDs.

Manually confirmed settings take precedence over an older descriptive suggestion. Reconcile the color/type plan when changes conflict; preserve concept and task order. A custom scene must keep its source and assets in the actual delivered project. Do not fall back to a generic scaffold when the renderer or asset handoff cannot represent the chosen composition.

## Acceptance

Before presenting or declaring completion, inspect an actual render and answer with evidence:

1. Can a viewer see what this project is for, what to do next and at least two recognizable visual traits?
2. Do color, type, region scale and content cooperate? Is visual emphasis distributed deliberately beyond a changed button?
3. Are the alternatives different in useful composition or reading order, rather than only names, hue or blur?
4. Are cards and rows proportional to their content, with coherent shell joins, intentional peer edges and action anchors? Is there accidental dead space or a wall of identical boxes?
5. Is the default result coordinated without opening advanced parameters? Can the person refine it in ordinary language?
6. Do contrast, text, icons, controls, task states and responsive behavior survive desktop plus a content-pressure narrow view? Is the chosen idea preserved at that width?
7. Do confirmation, optional prompt and implemented source retain the current intent and exact choices?

A successful build, passing token contrast, many customization options or a high score does not answer these visual questions. Prefer an independent reviewer where supported. Require a new pass for visible defects, lost intent or an unsuitable generic fallback; describe taste tradeoffs and unverified scope honestly. Do not force an expressive style when the user explicitly selected restraint.
